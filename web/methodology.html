Methodology

Data Sources and Statistical Definitions

This page explains how ReviewTime collects, cleans, aggregates, and interprets review-time data in Phase 1, and what the current numbers should and should not be used for.

Data Sources

  • Phase 1 uses article-level timing metadata extracted from public sources such as PubMed, PMC, and related publisher metadata.
  • The primary time points are `received`, `revised`, and `accepted`.
  • Each record preserves provenance fields such as `source_channel`, `source_url`, and `confidence_score`.
  • The public site serves static aggregated snapshots and does not depend on a live backend.

What Phase 1 Shows

  • Journal-level median review time, P25, P75, IQR, minimum, and maximum.
  • 3 / 6 / 12 / 24 month trends and the change between recent 6 months and recent 24 months.
  • Coverage window, source mix, sample count, and median confidence.
  • Comparison views across journals, publishers, and subjects where metadata is available.

Core Metrics

Metric Definition
Median review timeThe median of `days_received_to_accepted`, used as the primary speed indicator.
P25 / P75The 25th and 75th percentiles, describing the middle 50% of the sample.
IQR`P75 - P25`, used as a simple stability measure. Lower IQR means more consistency.
Trend change`recent 6-month median - recent 24-month median`; negative means faster recently, positive means slower recently.
Revision shareThe proportion of records that include revision information.
Revision to acceptThe median of `days_revised_last_to_accepted`, reflecting the final-stage handling time after the last revision.

Cleaning Rules

  • Only records with usable journal keys and valid dates are included in public aggregates.
  • `days_received_to_accepted <= 0` and values above `1000` are excluded from default public statistics.
  • `journal_label` is preferred as the journal key, with fallback to `journal_name`.
  • Dates are normalised to `YYYY-MM-DD`, and trends are grouped by accepted month.

What Phase 1 Does Not Expose

  • No public article-level timelines, DOI lists, or raw event rows are shown on the site.
  • No review report counts, review-quality scores, or editor ratings are fabricated from incomplete fields.
  • ReviewTime should not be interpreted as acceptance probability or editor friendliness.
  • For journals without complete public history, future phases will use overview mode rather than inventing missing detail.

How to Read the Results

  • Larger sample sizes generally support more robust conclusions.
  • Lower IQR usually means more stable handling time.
  • Recent trend should be interpreted together with the coverage window rather than in isolation.
  • Comparisons are most meaningful within similar subjects, publishers, and journal positions.
